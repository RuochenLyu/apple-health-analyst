## Design Context

### Users

People reviewing their own Apple Health export, from everyday users to people
who train regularly across many sports. They open a private, offline report on
desktop or mobile to understand long-term context, identify what is worth
monitoring, and prepare better questions or short-horizon behavior changes.
The interface must not assume boxing, endurance training, or advanced sports
science knowledge.

### Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, and evidence-led. The report should feel like a careful
open-source analysis instrument: transparent about source coverage and
uncertainty, direct without sounding clinical, and useful without pretending
to diagnose or coach.

### Aesthetic Direction

Refined editorial utility. Use a quiet light theme, strong typographic
hierarchy, restrained health-domain accents, precise charts, and generous
reading space. Preserve the single-file offline and print-friendly design.
Avoid gamified fitness dashboards, fake precision, glossy card grids,
decorative sparklines, Apple-product imitation, medical-alarm styling, and
generic neon or gradient-heavy AI aesthetics.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the judgment and next priority; let supporting evidence follow.
2. Make uncertainty, sample coverage, time windows, and device limitations
   visible without letting caveats overwhelm the report.
3. Use color to identify domains and status, never as the only carrier of
   meaning; target WCAG AA contrast and preserve keyboard, reduced-motion,
   mobile, and print usability.
4. Prefer a few meaningful visual groups over nested cards or repeated
   summaries. Every heading, metric, chart, and sentence must earn its place.
5. Keep Chinese and English structurally equivalent while allowing each
   language natural line length, terminology, and rhythm.
