What do these metrics mean?
42-Day Load Baseline (CTL)
A 42-day exponentially weighted average of estimated training load. It describes your longer-term personal load baseline in MET-minutes; it does not directly measure fitness.
7-Day Recent Load (ATL)
A 7-day exponentially weighted average of estimated training load. It describes how concentrated the latest week has been; it does not directly measure fatigue.
Load Balance (TSB)
TSB = CTL − ATL. It only shows whether recent estimated load is below or above your 42-day baseline. This report does not apply absolute thresholds borrowed from other training systems.
MET-minutes
Unit of training load. 1 MET ≈ sitting still. Running ≈ 8.5 MET, walking ≈ 3.5 MET, strength ≈ 5 MET. A 60-minute run ≈ 510 MET-minutes.
Recovery Context
Combines load direction with available sleep, HRV, and resting-heart-rate signals. It is a data cue, not a direct measurement of recovery or permission to add volume.

Data Limits & Boundaries

  • Training load is estimated from recorded duration and available MET values; it is not a laboratory measure.

  • Unrecorded exercise, illness, travel, and subjective exertion are not represented in the export.
